# Your First 30 Days With This Book

The Rosetta Stone gets you to the right chapter when you already have a problem. This page is for the opposite situation: you're starting from zero, nothing's on fire, and sixteen chapters is too many to tackle at once. Here's what a company starting from exactly nowhere actually did.

Ondas Digital didn't sit down and read this book cover to cover before acting. Almost everything that got fixed, got fixed because something forced the question, a client's security questionnaire, an incident, a near-miss. If you've read Ondas's story across Part Two, you'll notice this thirty-day path doesn't follow that same order. That's deliberate: what follows is the proactive version, what Ondas would have done with thirty quiet days and no incident forcing the sequence, not a record of what actually happened to them. The Priority Guide, below, uses this same proactive logic.

**Days 1–5: find out what you don't know**
Run Chapter 14's discovery routine, at minimum the OAuth/SSO grant review, the cheapest of the four methods and the one every company can run without new tooling. You will find tools nobody remembers approving. That's the point, not a failure. Feed what you find straight into Chapter 1, if anything turns up abandoned, decommission it properly rather than leaving it half-discovered.

**Days 6–12: write the policy that doesn't exist yet**
Build Chapter 4's tiered AUP for whatever tools Chapter 14 surfaced. Don't aim for comprehensive, aim for real: a handful of tools, honestly tiered, with actual training booked rather than a document nobody will read. This is also the moment to start Chapter 8's seam inventory for anything that calls a third-party AI tool or agent, even a simple one, since most companies this size have at least one seam they've never written down.

**Days 13–18: get ready before you need to be**
Build Chapter 6's clock table for the regimes that actually apply to you, don't guess, check. Pair it with Chapter 7's authority ladder: name the handful of actions that never need a meeting, and the handful of triggers that skip the ladder entirely and go straight to whoever holds real authority. Ondas built both of these only after an incident forced the question. You don't have to wait for yours.

**Days 19–24: check the parts that only apply if they apply to you**
Run Chapter 11's provider status check on anything you've built on top of a licensed model, fine-tuned, rebranded, or repurposed. If you use or sell any AI system that screens, ranks, or scores people, start Chapter 10's testing allocation, and be honest in the "gap identified" field rather than optimistic. If an automated score of yours drives someone else's real decision, Chapter 5's draws-strongly test tells you whether that's now a legal question, not just an operational one.

**Days 25–30: close the two gaps nobody thinks about until it's too late**
One conversation with your insurance broker, Chapter 13, confirms whether your policy still covers what you assume it covers. Fifteen minutes checking any AI vendor you'd genuinely struggle to lose, Chapter 12, tells you whether you could actually migrate if you had to. Neither of these feels urgent on day 25. Both are considerably cheaper to check now than to discover during an actual claim or an actual shutdown notice.

**After day 30**
Chapter 16's synthesis only works once the other chapters have something in them. Run it on one real system at the end of the month, not your best-documented one, your most representative one, and expect an honest partial answer rather than a clean pass. Ondas's own synthesis, in Chapter 16, still has two open gaps with dates attached rather than a tidy yes. That's what month one actually looks like, for everyone.

Chapters 2, 3, 9, and 15 aren't in this thirty-day path on purpose, they matter, but they assume the ground underneath them, a working inventory, a real AUP, is already there. Come back to them once it is.
