# Chapter 3: Naming the Kind of Human Participation You Actually Have

*Companion to "The Last Human Signature"*

The essay argued that a signature was never proof of comprehension, only of identity, and that "human oversight: yes" on an inventory form carries the same weakness. This chapter replaces that checkbox with something that can't be answered in one word.

## Worked example

**System:** AI-drafted client risk memos (RISK-AI-009)
**Tiers assigned:** Tier 2 (Bounded review) — senior analyst checks the memo's risk classification and headline figures against source data, explicitly does not re-derive the underlying model output. Also Tier 3 (Procedural sign-off) — a partner countersigns before the memo leaves the building, no independent check performed, no instance on record of a partner declining to sign
**Note:** the bounded review is real and has caught two miscategorisations in the last year. The partner sign-off has never once been withheld. Worth knowing which of the two is actually doing the work.

Two tiers on one system, and the honest picture that produces, a real check that works, sitting next to a formality that looks identical to it on any org chart, is exactly the distinction "human oversight: yes" was hiding.

## The tiers

A system can carry more than one at once. Tag every tier that genuinely applies, not the most flattering one.

**Tier 1 — Evaluative review.** A named person examines the output against evidence they independently hold, with time and competence to disagree, and disagreement has actually happened before in this workflow.

**Tier 2 — Bounded review.** Scope deliberately narrowed to what's actually assessable; everything outside it is explicitly labelled not-reviewed, not silently assumed covered. Only works if the boundary is written down, not just felt.

**Tier 3 — Procedural sign-off.** A human touches the record, signs, clicks, checks a box, but no independent evaluation occurs, and disagreement has never once been exercised. Looks identical to Tier 1 on paper.

**Tier 4 — No human touchpoint.** Fully automated, nothing to argue with either way.

**Tier 5 — Shared or ambiguous.** Two or more people or systems each assume the other is doing the evaluative work, so no single accountable reviewer can actually be named. Needs a free-text note, this tier describes a gap, not a control.

## Who fills this in, and when

Filled by someone who has actually watched the review happen, not by whoever wrote the original process document describing how it's supposed to work. The gap between those two is the entire point of this chapter: a process document will confidently claim Tier 1 for a review that has quietly become Tier 3 over eighteen months, because nobody ever had reason to test whether disagreement was still possible.

The tell to watch for: if the answer to "when did this reviewer last actually disagree with the output" is "I don't know" or "a while ago," downgrade the tier until someone can answer that question with a date.
