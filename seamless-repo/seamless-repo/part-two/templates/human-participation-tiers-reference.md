## Human Participation Tiers — Inventory Field

A single AI system can carry more than one tier at once (a human may genuinely evaluate the flag but only procedurally sign the output that follows), so this field is a checklist of applicable tiers per system, not a single dropdown pick.

**Tier 1 — Evaluative review.** A named person examines the system's output against evidence they independently have access to, with enough time and domain competence to plausibly disagree with it, and disagreement is a live option that has actually been exercised before. Note: this is the only tier the traditional idea of human oversight actually describes, and it is rare precisely because it costs the time the other tiers were invented to avoid.

**Tier 2 — Bounded review.** A named person evaluates the output against a scope deliberately narrowed to what they can actually assess in the time available, everything outside that scope is explicitly not reviewed and is labelled as such, rather than silently assumed covered. Note: this is the tier the Signature piece's "narrower, honest ask" describes, its practical value depends entirely on the boundary being written down and shared, not just felt by the reviewer.

**Tier 3 — Procedural sign-off.** A human touches the record, a signature, a click, a checkbox, but no evaluation against independent evidence occurs, and no disagreement has ever been exercised in this workflow's history. This is what the Signature piece calls the accident of comprehension after the friction that used to produce it has gone.

**Tier 4 — No human touchpoint.** Fully automated, no signature, click, or review step anywhere in the path from input to consequence.

**Tier 5 — Shared or ambiguous.** Two or more people, roles, or systems each assume the other is doing the evaluative work, and no single accountable reviewer can be named. Needs a free-text note describing the ambiguity itself, since this tier is a description of a gap, not a description of a control.
