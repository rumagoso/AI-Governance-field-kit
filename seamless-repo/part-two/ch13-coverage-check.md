# Naming What Your Policy Actually Excludes Now

*Companion to "Nobody Told the Renewal"*

## Worked example

**Policy:** Ondas's general commercial liability, renewed annually, handled by Mariana directly since Ondas has no dedicated finance function
**AI-specific exclusion present:** Yes, confirmed 3 March 2027 after this exercise prompted Mariana to actually ask the broker rather than assume the renewal packet was the same as last year's. ISO endorsement form CG 40 47 had been attached at the previous renewal, eleven months earlier, without anyone flagging it
**What this excludes:** Claims arising from Ondas's use of generative AI in client work, per the broker's plain-language summary once actually asked for one
**Alternative coverage in place:** None, identified as a real gap, eleven months after it had quietly existed
**Named owner of closing the gap:** Mariana Cabral, getting a standalone AI liability quote before the bank client's chatbot (Chapters 8 and 11) ships anything further
**Last checked:** 3 March 2027

The gap being found is the point of this exercise, not a failure of it. A policy review that always concludes "we're covered" without ever finding a real exclusion probably isn't being read closely enough.

## Building the check

**Policy**
Every policy that could plausibly be assumed to cover AI-related liability: general liability, cyber, technology E&O, D&O.

**AI-specific exclusion present**
Confirmed by form number where the broker can provide one, not assumed absent because nobody mentioned it. Silence in a renewal conversation is not evidence of continued coverage.

**What this excludes**
In plain language, not the policy's own legal wording. If the broker can't explain it in a sentence a non-specialist would understand, that's worth flagging on its own.

**Alternative coverage in place**
Whether a standalone AI liability product, or some other mechanism, actually closes the gap the exclusion opened. "We're looking into it" is not coverage.

**Named owner of closing the gap**
One person accountable for either obtaining alternative coverage or making an informed, documented decision to accept the risk uninsured, not a gap that just persists because nobody owns the follow-up.

## Who fills this in, and when

Filled by whoever manages insurance relationships, at every renewal, not as a one-time check. The exclusion landscape is moving quickly, forms that didn't exist eighteen months ago are now standard across major carriers, and a check done once in 2026 will not reflect what's in a 2028 renewal without being repeated.

The failure mode specific to this chapter: treating "the broker didn't mention it" as equivalent to "we checked." Brokers are not uniformly flagging these exclusions as headline changes yet, which means the absence of a warning is not evidence of the absence of a gap. The only reliable check is asking the specific question and getting a specific, form-numbered answer.
