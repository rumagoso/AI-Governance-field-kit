# Meet Ondas Digital

Every chapter in Part Two uses the same company. Not because one company's story is unusually dramatic, but because most companies this size, small enough that nobody has a dedicated compliance function, big enough that "just be careful" has stopped working, are dealing with some version of every problem in this book at the same time, usually without realising the problems are connected.

**Ondas Digital** is a 24-person digital marketing and software boutique based in Leiria, Portugal. Founded in 2019 by **Mariana Cabral**, it grew from four people to twenty-four in five years by doing good client work and saying yes to opportunities as they came. Governance did not grow at the same rate. This is not a criticism specific to Ondas. It is close to the median pattern for a company this size and this age.

**The team you'll meet across these chapters:**

- **Mariana Cabral** — Founder and Managing Director. Signs off on most client-facing work, including AI-drafted proposals, without always having time to read them closely.
- **Tiago Vento** — Head of Delivery. Owns most operational decisions and reviews AI-assisted content before it reaches clients.
- **Sofia Reis** — Marketing Operations Lead. Runs the campaigns that increasingly involve AI-generated content.
- **Bruno Alves** — Contract developer. Builds the client-facing product features, including a chatbot for Ondas's largest client, a regional bank, that turns out to touch more of this book than anyone expected.
- **Inês Duarte** — People Lead, hired once headcount passed twenty. Adopts an AI-powered applicant-screening tool as hiring scales.
- A CTO-as-a-service consultant, brought in one day a quarter, who is usually the first person to notice something's been quietly wrong for a while.

Ondas's clients are mid-market European companies. One, a regional bank, brings its own weight: contractual security questionnaires, tighter incident-notification obligations, and, without anyone at Ondas fully registering it, exposure to rules built for a very different kind of company than a twenty-four person agency in Leiria.

The essays in Part One keep their real anchors, real cases, real rulings, real incidents, because that's what makes their arguments hold up. Ondas exists for a different reason: to show what applying each chapter's tool actually looks like inside one small, ordinary, recognisable company, the same one, chapter after chapter, so that by the time you reach the synthesis exercise in Chapter 16, you're not imagining how the pieces fit together. You've already watched them.
